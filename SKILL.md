# Webmatic Sales & Lead Qualification

Use this skill when Webmatic receives a new lead, client inquiry, sales opportunity, pricing question, proposal request, or follow-up opportunity.

The objective is to identify quality opportunities, understand the client's actual need, protect Webmatic profitability, and move qualified leads toward an appropriate next action.

Do not treat every lead as equally valuable.

## 1. Capture Lead Information

Collect available information:

- Client name
- Business name
- Industry
- Website
- Location/market
- Contact method
- Requested service
- Business objective
- Current problem
- Desired outcome
- Budget
- Timeline
- Decision maker
- Existing website/platform
- Technical requirements
- Urgency
- Previous provider, if relevant
- Additional requirements

Do not invent missing information.

## 2. Understand the Business Problem

Identify:

- What problem does the client have?
- Why does it matter?
- What happens if it is not solved?
- What result does the client want?
- What service could realistically solve it?
- Is the requested solution actually appropriate?

Do not immediately quote a price before understanding the scope when the requirements are unclear.

## 3. Lead Qualification

Evaluate:

### Need
Does the client have a genuine business or technical need?

### Fit
Does the requested work match Webmatic's capabilities?

### Budget
Is the expected budget realistic for the required scope?

### Timeline
Is the requested timeline achievable?

### Scope
Is the scope clear enough to estimate?

### Decision Maker
Is the person communicating able to approve the project?

### Value
Could the project create meaningful business value?

### Risk
Are there unreasonable expectations, unclear requirements, payment concerns, or technical risks?

## 4. Lead Categories

Classify leads as:

### High Potential
Strong need, good fit, realistic scope, reasonable commercial opportunity, and clear next step.

### Needs Qualification
Potentially suitable but important information is missing.

### Low Potential
Limited budget, poor fit, unrealistic expectations, unclear scope, or low commercial value.

### Not Suitable
Outside Webmatic capabilities, inappropriate requirements, unacceptable risk, or clearly unsuitable business conditions.

Do not reject a lead based on assumptions.

## 5. Discovery Questions

Use only the questions necessary to understand the opportunity.

Useful questions include:

### Business
- What does your business do?
- Who are your target customers?
- What is the main goal of the project?

### Current Situation
- Do you already have a website/store?
- What platform are you currently using?
- What problems are you experiencing?

### Requirements
- What pages/features do you need?
- How many products/pages are involved?
- Do you need payment, shipping, booking, forms, integrations, or email?

### Timeline
- When would you like the project completed?
- Is there a specific launch deadline?

### Budget
- Do you have an approximate budget range?
- Is the budget already approved?

### Decision
- Who will approve the project?
- Are there other stakeholders?

Do not ask unnecessary questions when the scope is already clear.

## 6. Service Matching

Match the lead to the appropriate Webmatic service.

Examples:

- Business website → WordPress
- Online store → WooCommerce or Shopify
- Existing WordPress issue → WordPress troubleshooting
- Existing Shopify issue → Shopify specialist
- Slow website → Performance optimization
- Hacked website → Security/malware recovery
- Search visibility problem → SEO
- Social growth → Social media/marketing
- Website problem discovery → Website audit
- Domain/hosting/email problem → Technical consulting

If multiple solutions are possible, explain the trade-offs.

## 7. Pricing

Use the official Webmatic pricing stored in `webmatic-pricing`.

Never invent or silently change official base prices.

When scope exceeds a standard package:

- Explain the scope difference.
- Identify additional work.
- Estimate effort.
- Prepare a custom quote where appropriate.

Third-party costs should be clearly separated.

Examples:

- Hosting
- Domain
- Premium plugins
- Premium themes
- Shopify subscription
- Shopify apps
- Payment gateway fees
- Google Workspace
- External services

## 8. Price Objections

When a client says the price is too high:

1. Clarify what they expected.
2. Confirm whether the issue is budget or perceived value.
3. Explain what is included.
4. Identify the most important requirements.
5. Reduce scope before reducing price.
6. Offer a smaller appropriate package when possible.

Do not automatically discount.

Do not undermine Webmatic pricing.

Do not pressure the client.

## 9. Scope Protection

Watch for requests that expand the original scope.

Examples:

- Additional pages
- Additional products
- Additional functionality
- New integrations
- New designs
- Additional revisions
- Content creation
- SEO work
- Marketing work
- Technical fixes unrelated to the original project

Determine whether the request is:

- Included
- A normal revision
- Additional scope
- Separate service
- New project

Get approval before substantial additional work.

## 10. Proposal Recommendation

When a qualified lead is ready for a proposal, structure the recommendation around:

- Client objective
- Recommended solution
- Scope
- Deliverables
- Timeline
- Price
- Payment terms
- Included revisions
- Exclusions
- Third-party costs
- Client responsibilities
- Maintenance/ongoing support
- Next step

Do not create promises that are not supported by the approved scope.

## 11. Follow-Up Workflow

Use a professional follow-up sequence.

### First Follow-Up
Confirm whether the client reviewed the proposal or has questions.

### Second Follow-Up
Clarify any outstanding requirements or concerns.

### Final Follow-Up
Politely close the active follow-up loop while leaving the door open for future work.

Do not spam clients.

Do not create fake urgency.

## 12. Sales Pipeline

Use:

New Lead
→ Qualified
→ Discovery
→ Proposal
→ Follow-Up
→ Negotiation
→ Won
→ Project Setup
→ Delivery
→ Maintenance / Retainer

Also allow:

Lost
Not Suitable
On Hold

## 13. Lead Prioritization

Prioritize based on:

- Revenue potential
- Profitability
- Service fit
- Urgency
- Probability of conversion based on actual evidence
- Recurring revenue potential
- Client quality
- Technical risk
- Time required

Do not claim a lead is likely to convert without evidence.

## 14. Recurring Revenue Opportunities

After understanding the project, identify genuine opportunities for:

- Website maintenance
- Security monitoring
- Backups
- Updates
- Hosting management
- SEO
- Content updates
- Social media
- Technical support
- E-commerce management

Only recommend recurring services when there is a genuine client need.

## 15. Referral Opportunities

After successful delivery, consider:

- Referral request
- Review/testimonial
- Case study
- Additional business services
- Related services for the same client

Do not pressure clients.

## 16. Sales Output

For a new lead, use:

### Lead Summary
Brief description.

### Client Need
What the client actually needs.

### Recommended Service
Best-fit Webmatic service.

### Qualification
- Need
- Fit
- Budget
- Timeline
- Scope
- Risk

### Lead Category
High Potential / Needs Qualification / Low Potential / Not Suitable

### Missing Information
Only essential missing information.

### Recommended Next Action
The next practical sales step.

### Commercial Opportunity
Potential service, upsell, or recurring opportunity where justified.

## 17. Important Rules

- Never invent client information.
- Never invent budgets.
- Never invent requirements.
- Never promise results.
- Never guarantee rankings, conversions, revenue, or traffic.
- Never pressure or manipulate clients.
- Do not automatically discount.
- Reduce scope before reducing price.
- Protect Webmatic profitability.
- Protect against scope creep.
- Separate facts from assumptions.
- Use official Webmatic pricing.
- Recommend the next practical action.
